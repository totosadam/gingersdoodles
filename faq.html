Frequently asked questions

The questions families ask us most. If yours is not here, email us — we answer every message ourselves.

How do I reserve a puppy?
Start with the puppy application. Once we have talked, a reservation fee secures your pick spot on the waitlist for an upcoming litter and is applied to your puppy’s total.
When can my puppy come home?
Puppies go home around eight weeks, once they are weaned, vet-checked, and have started their vaccinations. Until then you’ll get regular photos and updates as they grow.
How do you choose which puppy is ours?
Selection happens after temperament evaluations at about seven weeks, so you choose with real information in hand. Waitlist families pick in order, and we help match personality to your household, energy level, and experience.
Are these dogs good with kids and allergies?
Wonderfully so. Australian Labradoodles and Bernedoodles are intelligent, gentle, and eager to please, which makes them favorite family dogs. Their low-shedding, low-dander coats are also a great fit for many households with mild allergies.
Do you offer a health guarantee and ongoing support?
Yes. Our guarantee covers genetic or inherited disease diagnosed within the first two years, and every puppy is raised from health-tested parents. Support doesn’t end at pickup — we’re a message away for the whole life of your dog, and we ask that any Ginger’s Doodles pup always come back to us if you ever cannot keep them.
Can our puppy be delivered to us?
Most families come to us for go-home day, which we love — it’s a chance to meet in person. If you’re further out, ask us about ground or air transport options and current fees.
What does a puppy cost?
Pricing depends on the pairing and is confirmed when a litter is announced. We will always tell you the full amount, the reservation fee, and what is included before you commit to anything.
Do you require spay or neuter?
Yes — all of our puppies go home on a companion contract with a spay or neuter agreement at the age our vet recommends for their size. Breeding rights would have to be discussed and are sold at a higher price.
